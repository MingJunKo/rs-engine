Works from the GitHub phone app too.

How it works

σ (sigma) move — today's % change divided by the typical daily move over the last 20 sessions. A 3σ day is about 0.3% likely under a normal distribution; markets have fatter tails, so the page counts how often it really happened.

Price signals: 1-day ≤ −2.5σ · 5-day ≤ −2.5σ · ≥ 2.5σ below the 20-day average · ≥ 3 ATRs below the 50-day MA · RSI(2) ≤ 5 · 5+ down days. Stretched-up mirrors: ≥ +2.5σ, ≥ 4 ATRs above the 50-day MA (Jeff Sun's extension limit), RSI(2) ≥ 95, 7+ up days.

Volatility: VIX ≥ 30 · VIX ≥ 1.4× its 20-day average (spike) · VIX above VIX3M (curve inverted — short-term fear above longer-term, typical of capitulation) · VIX ≤ 12 (complacency).

Breadth (from the RS Engine's ~2,800-stock universe): % of stocks above the 40-day MA ≤ 20 (Stockbee's T2108 idea) · ≥ 5% of stocks down 4%+ in a day (Stockbee's breadth thrust/panic count) · 5-day ratio of 4% up vs 4% down days ≤ 0.5 · a McClellan-style oscillator (19/39-day EMAs of net advances) ≤ −60. This is a proxy for NYMO/NAMO, which need exchange-wide advance/decline data that isn't on Yahoo.

Statistics: episodes are the first day a signal fires, ignoring repeats within 10 sessions. Forward returns are SPY or QQQ from that day's close. Compare them with the "any day" line — the edge is the difference. History: SPY since 1993, QQQ since 1999, VIX3M since 2007, breadth ~2 years.

Alerts (Telegram): when oversold signals come from at least two families, or when a big one fires for the first time (σ day, VIX ≥ 30, inverted curve, T2108 ≤ 20, 4% panic). A weekly read every Saturday morning.
